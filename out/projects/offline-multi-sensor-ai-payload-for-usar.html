Research prototype

Offline Multi-Sensor AI Payload for Autonomous Victim Detection and Voice Interaction in Search and Rescue Robots

A modular ROS 2 payload combining thermal-triggered YOLOv11n, FMCW radar, thermal vision, offline speech and language models, and long-range telemetry on a mobile rescue robot.

USAREdge ComputingYOLOv11FMCW RadarThermal VisionOffline LLMLoRaROS 2

Overview

This research prototype was designed to keep perception, decision support, voice interaction, and communication available when cloud connectivity is unreliable or unavailable. The primary validation platform combines a TurtleBot3 Waffle Pi with an NVIDIA Jetson TX2 and STM32F411 sensor hub.

The engineering work integrates visual and thermal sensing, FMCW bio-radar, ROS 2 navigation, edge inference, offline human-robot interaction, and hybrid Wi-Fi/LoRa telemetry into one modular controller. Its Wake-on-Suspect and SIGKILL-based state-management strategies focus compute resources on the active mission task while protecting a memory-constrained embedded platform.

Mission context

Urban search-and-rescue operations can disrupt power, cellular coverage, and network infrastructure at exactly the moment autonomous robots must continue to sense, navigate, and communicate. This project develops a modular edge-AI payload that keeps the full victim-detection and voice-interaction workflow on the robot rather than depending on cloud services.

Integrated system

The system was validated primarily on a TurtleBot3 Waffle Pi and uses an NVIDIA Jetson TX2 as its edge-compute unit, with an STM32F411 acting as a dedicated sensor hub. The architecture integrates ROS 2 navigation with multiple sensing modalities and a local communication stack.

  • Visual perception: a TensorRT FP16-optimised YOLOv11n detector supports person detection in the on-board camera stream.
  • Presence confirmation: FMCW bio-radar and thermal sensing provide redundant cues when conventional visual detection is uncertain.
  • Offline dialogue: the voice pipeline links Faster-Whisper speech recognition, Gemma 3 language inference, and Piper text-to-speech without internet access.
  • Long-range telemetry: hybrid Wi-Fi and LoRa SX1278 communication carries mission data across challenging operating conditions.

Resource-aware autonomy

Wake-on-Suspect operation

A thermal-triggered protocol keeps the high-power detection pipeline inactive until a suspect heat signature requires confirmation. This reduces unnecessary GPU use while retaining a responsive investigation path.

SIGKILL-based state management

To run large models on a memory-constrained Jetson TX2, the Master State Machine explicitly retires the outgoing model process before loading the next task. This approach avoids overlap in model memory demand and makes mission-mode transitions deterministic.

Measured validation

  • YOLOv11n: 96.1% mAP@50 at 26.3 FPS with TensorRT FP16, a 7.5× inference-speed improvement over the reported baseline.
  • FMCW bio-radar: approximately 94% human-presence detection in controlled laboratory conditions.
  • Voice interaction: the complete local speech-to-speech pipeline measured approximately 17 seconds end-to-end on the Jetson TX2; this is a current optimisation target rather than a cloud-dependent fallback.
  • Memory resilience: 0% out-of-memory crash rate with a 71% reduction in mode-switching latency.
  • Operational efficiency: the Wake-on-Suspect protocol extended operational runtime by 35% compared with continuous always-on inference.
  • Telemetry reach: reliable delivery was measured up to 600 m through reinforced concrete indoors and 2.5 km line-of-sight outdoors.
The platform is designed as a hardware-agnostic controller that can be adapted to ROS 2-compatible mobile bases after the primary TurtleBot validation.

Engineering contribution

The project brings perception, edge computing, safety-oriented resource management, navigation support, and human-robot dialogue into a single deployable rescue-robot payload. Its central contribution is not a single model: it is the orchestrated, offline operation of complementary sensing and AI subsystems under embedded hardware constraints.
