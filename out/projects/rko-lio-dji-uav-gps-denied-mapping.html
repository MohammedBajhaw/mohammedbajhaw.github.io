Research prototype

Demonstrating the Efficiency of RKO-LIO on a DJI UAV Platform Utilizing Drone Built-in IMU for GPS-Denied Mapping

A DJI Matrice M350 RTK payload combining a Velodyne VLP-16, NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX, and the aircraft’s 200 Hz built-in IMU through DJI E-Port to validate RKO-LIO mapping performance in GPS-denied conditions.

UAVRKO-LIOLiDAR-Inertial OdometryROS 2Sensor FusionGPS-denied NavigationDJI E-Port

Overview

This project demonstrates a practical integration of RKO-LIO on a DJI Matrice M350 RTK platform. The payload couples a Velodyne VLP-16 LiDAR with an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX (16 GB) and accesses the drone built-in IMU at 200 Hz through DJI E-Port.

The work focuses on reducing sensor cost, payload weight, and mechanical complexity while retaining robust LiDAR-inertial odometry for outdoor mapping where GPS cannot be relied on. Hardware power and communication circuits were developed around UART and DC-DC interfaces, and the software stack was profiled during a dynamic flight test.

Project objective

This project validates RKO-LIO on a DJI Matrice M350 RTK platform for GPS-denied 3D mapping. Instead of adding a separate external inertial sensor, the design receives the aircraft built-in IMU at 200 Hz through DJI E-Port and fuses it with Velodyne VLP-16 LiDAR data on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX.

Payload and integration

The system is built around a DJI Matrice M350 RTK, NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX (16 GB), and Velodyne VLP-16. The software pipeline includes DJI PSDK ROS 2 integration, LiDAR pre-processing, and RKO-LIO optimisation. Hardware design uses the DJI E-Port for IMU access and implements the necessary UART and DC-DC power interfaces for a practical airborne payload.

  • Airframe: DJI Matrice M350 RTK provides the aerial platform and built-in inertial measurements.
  • Perception: Velodyne VLP-16 supplies the 3D point-cloud stream for mapping.
  • Edge compute: Jetson Orin NX performs the full ROS 2 and RKO-LIO workload on board.
  • Navigation context: the system targets reconstruction quality in GPS-denied conditions rather than depending on GNSS localisation.

Robust mapping rationale

RKO-LIO incorporates robust kernel optimisation to reduce the influence of outliers that can arise from dynamic objects and changing vegetation. In outdoor UAV flight, this matters because vehicles, moving foliage, and other non-static observations can otherwise distort the estimated structural map.

Flight-test results

  • Trajectory: the dynamic outdoor flight covered 432.72 m.
  • Structural accuracy: average relative structural error was 1.57%, with a reported maximum of 2.47%.
  • Mapping scope: the experiment reconstructed approximately 5,673.91 m² of area and 190,343 m³ of volume.
  • Edge resource use: the full stack averaged 25.79% CPU use and 1.99 GB RAM, with a reported peak of 2.01 GB.
Using the drone built-in IMU through DJI E-Port helped reduce sensor cost, payload weight, and mechanical complexity while maintaining a robust mapping pipeline.

Engineering contribution

The main contribution is a field-oriented UAV integration that links DJI platform interfaces, embedded power and communications hardware, ROS 2 middleware, LiDAR-inertial estimation, and performance profiling. The architecture leaves a path for future real-time environmental analysis on the generated map while keeping the mapping workload feasible on edge hardware.
